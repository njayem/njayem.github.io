Research Motivation

Speech changes can emerge early in Parkinson’s disease, offering opportunities for accessible, non-invasive screening and remote monitoring. Yet strong predictive performance does not necessarily indicate that a model has learned the underlying signatures of disease. It may instead rely on demographic characteristics, recording conditions, or other sources of variation unrelated to pathology. HEARD places this distinction at the centre of its research, examining not only what these models learn, but what constitutes sufficient evidence to justify their use in clinical practice.

Celebs4PD: A Demographically Matched Corpus of Longitudinal, In-the-Wild Speech for Responsible Parkinson’s Detection

A demographically matched corpus of longitudinal, in-the-wild speech designed to investigate whether domain-relevant pretraining improves clinical Parkinson’s disease detection and reduces reliance on scarce clinical data.

Paper 3 · In preparation

Confounders Before Conclusions: Auditing What Speech Foundation Models Hear in Parkinson’s Disease

A systematic investigation of the extent to which speech foundation models rely on disease-related speech characteristics rather than demographic, linguistic, and recording-related confounders, with an emphasis on robustness, generalisability, and the validity of the auditing methodology itself.

Paper 4 · In preparation

Participatory Foresight for Responsible Deployment of Speech Biomarkers in Parkinson Disease

A participatory investigation of the conditions under which speech biomarkers for Parkinson’s disease may be responsibly deployed, bringing together empirical evidence and the perspectives of those involved in, and affected by, their use.

Methodological Foundations

01Confounder-aware study designIndividuals with Parkinson’s disease and controls are matched on demographic and recording-related characteristics to mitigate known sources of confounding.
02Quantifying methodological trade-offsComparative training strategies examine the relationship between confounder control, data availability, and predictive performance.
03Auditing the auditControlled shortcut injection experiments assess the sensitivity of the auditing methodology to known sources of confounding.
04Independent external validationModels are evaluated across previously unseen datasets, recording environments and devices, with decision thresholds and calibration parameters held fixed.
05Transparency and reproducibilityEvaluation protocols are specified in advance, with systematic documentation of experimental decisions, random sampling, and model predictions.
06Participatory governanceThe conditions for responsible clinical deployment are informed by the perspectives, priorities, and lived experiences of those the technology is intended to serve.
