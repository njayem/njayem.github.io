Speech-based artificial intelligence offers new possibilities for accessible screening and monitoring of Parkinson’s disease. However, the legitimacy of its introduction into clinical practice rests on more than predictive accuracy. Questions of reliability, equity, privacy, and accountability carry distinct implications for patients, caregivers, and clinicians, whose perspectives must inform the conditions under which these technologies are developed and deployed.

This study uses participatory foresight to anticipate the consequences of introducing speech-based AI into clinical practice. Six plausible future scenarios, ranging from speech-based assessments during clinical consultations to remote monitoring and prediagnostic screening, will be grounded in empirical investigations of what speech models learn and how reliably they generalise to new datasets and settings. Speech-language pathologists, neurologists, responsible AI researchers, ethicists, people with Parkinson’s disease, and caregivers will engage with these scenarios through a survey and interviews.

By examining areas of consensus, competing values, and the conditions stakeholders consider necessary for clinical adoption, the study aims to establish a framework for the responsible deployment of speech biomarkers in Parkinson’s disease.
